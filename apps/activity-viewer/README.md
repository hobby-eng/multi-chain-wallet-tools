# Wallet Activity Viewer

This independent viewer integrates the official open-source Dash Platform Evo/WASM SDK and Dash Orchard fork without claiming upstream authorship or endorsement. See the repository [attribution](../../ATTRIBUTION.md) and [third-party notices](../../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

Original project code is released under the repository's [MIT License](../../LICENSE); embedded dependencies retain their separately listed licenses.

Network-enabled, read-only standalone viewer. Multi-Chain Edition has a Bitcoin / EVM networks / Dash / Other networks selector with Bitcoin selected by default. Bitcoin and Ethereum modes load current state and confirmed lifetime history for public addresses in Single or Batch mode. Ethereum Classic loads its balance, nonce and confirmed lifetime history the same way, on Mainnet only. The other EVM networks (BNB Chain, Polygon, Arbitrum One, OP Mainnet, Base, Avalanche C-Chain and Gnosis) load the current balance and nonce only, on Mainnet only (Testnet is disabled for them); their history is reported as unsupported, not as zero. The other networks Solana, XRP, Stellar, NEAR, Tron, Cosmos, Injective, Hedera and TON load the current balance of a public address (for NEAR, of the account a public key `ed25519:…` names; for Hedera, of the accounts an account ID, an EVM address or a public key names) on Mainnet only, with the account's sequence for XRP, Stellar, Cosmos and Injective, a TON wallet's seqno, the numbers of the Hedera accounts found, and the ledger, slot or block the provider read where it reports one; their history is reported as unsupported too. Litecoin, Dogecoin, Bitcoin Cash and Zcash are not offered: no public provider found for them answers a browser's requests at a usable rate. Dash mode supports Core, Platform payment-address, Platform Identity, and Orchard records; Auto classifies compatible inputs locally, while Advanced provides an explicit Dash record type. Dash Community Edition compiles and presents only that Dash workflow. Orchard batch mode fetches each proof-verified encrypted pool page once and scans it locally against every validated viewing key instead of repeating the network traversal. Build outputs: `dist/multi-chain-edition/activity-viewer/Wallet_Activity_Viewer.html` and `dist/dash-community-edition/activity-viewer/Dash_Community_Activity_Viewer.html`.

Bitcoin balance lookup uses bounded batches with rotating Blockchain.com and BlockCypher requests plus Blockstream.info/Mempool.space fallback; confirmed address history and block times come from the Esplora-compatible endpoints. Ethereum and every other EVM network's current balance and nonce are queried at an explicit block height from the network's own fixed JSON-RPC endpoint (PublicNode's; HebeBlock's `etc.etcdesktop.com` for Ethereum Classic), after it reports the network's chain id; different address queries can observe different heights, while the confirmed native-asset and internal-transfer history of Ethereum and Ethereum Classic comes from each network's own Blockscout (`eth.blockscout.com`, `etc.blockscout.com`). Provider responses are strictly validated, but they remain third-party indexed/RPC data rather than local consensus verification. Incomplete bounded history is labelled partial and its dates or totals are withheld where completeness cannot be established.

Each account coin is read from one public provider, from the build's account coin table: Solana `solana-rpc.publicnode.com` (PublicNode JSON-RPC, `getMultipleAccounts` and the latest signature at finalized commitment), XRP `xrplcluster.com` (XRPL Labs, `account_info` in the latest validated ledger), Stellar `horizon.stellar.org` (the Stellar Development Foundation's Horizon), NEAR `free.rpc.fastnear.com` (FastNEAR, `view_account` at final finality, the liquid amount without staked NEAR), Tron `api.trongrid.io` (TronGrid; frozen TRX is not read), Cosmos `cosmos-rest.publicnode.com` and Injective `injective-rest.publicnode.com` (PublicNode's Cosmos SDK REST gateways, the auth module's account and its bank balance; delegated coins are not read), Hedera `mainnet.mirrornode.hedera.com` (Hedera's public mainnet mirror node) and TON `toncenter.com` (TON Center's API v3 `walletInformation` answering from a liteserver, asked one request at a time 1.1 seconds after its previous answer, as it answers a client without a key about once a second; a rate-limited request is asked again once). An account that does not exist on its network shows a zero balance only when the provider says so in its own form (XRP's `actNotFound`, Stellar's 404 `not_found`, NEAR's `UNKNOWN_ACCOUNT`, Tron's empty list, the Cosmos SDK's gRPC NotFound, the mirror node's 404 or an empty list of a key's accounts, TON Center's `uninitialized` without a transaction or balance); any other answer fails the lookup. A Hedera lookup lists the numbers of the accounts it found with their balances and sums them: a public key finds every account it alone controls, one page of at most 100, and an ECDSA key also the account of its EVM address alias. In a batch, an account that two lookups reach counts once in the current balance, and the page says so.

Core uses the replaceable DashScan provider. Platform payment-address current balance/nonce is proof-verified through Evo SDK and indexed history is loaded from Dash Platform Explorer. Identity lookup accepts a Base58 Identity ID, an explicitly labelled `idhex:<64-hex Identity ID>`, an explicitly labelled `tx:<64-hex registration transition>`, the HASH160 fingerprint of a public key registered to the Identity, compressed ECDSA/BLS public key, or DPNS name with or without `.dash`; DAPI proofs establish current Identity state, nonce, all registered keys and DPNS names, while Explorer supplies clearly labelled auxiliary indexed history. A transaction lookup accepts only an Identity creation transition, locally checks the transition hash and decodes its owner before requesting that Identity from proof-verified DAPI. Bare 64-hex values remain blocked because they are indistinguishable from raw private keys. Registration time/hash come from the actual Identity creation transition rather than Explorer's latest-transition summary, and classic registrations decode their Dash Core asset-lock outpoint locally with the pinned Evo SDK. Identity results use local tabs for overview/names, keys, activity, documents, contracts, withdrawals, and tokens; changing tabs makes no network request. Owner transactions and transfer legs sharing a transaction hash are rendered as one ledger event. Orchard encrypted-note pages are proof-verified and decrypted locally with the pinned WASM scanner.

Mnemonic, WIF, extended-private-key, raw-private-key and structured private-material patterns are rejected and erased before the viewer connects to any public lookup provider. Public keys are converted to HASH160 locally, so the full key is not sent.

Batch inputs are all inspected for private material before the first connection is opened. In Dash mode, Auto recognizes unambiguous formats and supports `core:`, `platform:`, `identity:`, `orchard-fvk:`, `orchard-ivk:`, and `orchard-ovk:` prefixes where an explicit interpretation is needed. Bare 64-hex input remains blocked. Invalid Dash records fail independently, successful mixed-type results remain selectable without another request, and Dash CSV/XLSX/JSON export contains the complete batch plus per-input errors. CSV uses one compact summary row per resource followed by typed records; JSON groups state and history under each object. XLSX provides a Summary sheet and separate Addresses, Identities, Orchard, and Errors sheets when those categories are present. Single and Batch modes expose CSV/XLSX/JSON exports for every included coin adapter. Bitcoin, the EVM networks, and future public-address adapters use the normalized public-activity export; its `nonce` column keeps that name for compatibility and holds the account nonce for the EVM networks, the account sequence for XRP, Stellar, Cosmos and Injective and a TON wallet's seqno, and is empty for Bitcoin, Solana, NEAR, Tron and Hedera; its last column, `account_ids`, lists the Hedera accounts a lookup found and is empty for every other coin; Dash retains its detailed typed worksheets. Exact integer amounts are kept as text so JavaScript or spreadsheet software cannot round them. No export contains an Orchard viewing key.

Dash exports use [schema version 3](docs/EXPORT_SCHEMA.md): Core reported balance and accounting discrepancy have neutral names, transaction confirmations remain independent of lock flags, and partial Orchard scans never claim an established spendable balance or unspent note. Platform indexed history is admitted only for the requested resource and network; it remains separate from proof-backed current state.

The query control is fail-closed until a visible startup passport reports that the embedded Orchard runtime passed its deterministic public self-test, the public-input guard refused a phrase, a WIF, an extended private key, Ed25519 secret keys, DER private keys and secret seeds and passed public values, activity-state checks passed for exact accounting, partial spend refusal and Platform resource admission, and a real same-document Blob Worker completed a handshake. Multi-Chain builds also check Bitcoin and EVM addresses against BIP173, BIP350 and EIP-55 vectors before any lookup. The official Evo SDK uses an embedded Blob Worker for WASM compilation, so the reviewed connected CSP permits `worker-src blob:` while still forbidding external worker URLs. Its immutable inline application script is authorized by an exact build-time SHA-256 CSP hash rather than `unsafe-inline`. The package has no published read-only browser entry point; release verification therefore uses the TypeScript compiler AST to reject known SDK transfer, withdrawal, identity-write, and broadcast calls, including literal/computed property access, optional chains, and simple aliases. This is defense in depth over known write surfaces, not a type-level proof over future APIs. The passport also contains the exact embedded dependency versions/licenses; this information is not duplicated elsewhere in the standalone page.

```bash
pnpm build:activity-viewer
node apps/activity-viewer/scripts/verify-activity-viewer-artifact.mjs
```

Live provider checks are separate from deterministic builds; see the root [release procedure](../../RELEASING.md). Read [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) before pasting a viewing key.

Query controls, including Coin, remain locked until the active request settles. Clear and Cancel discard late responses. Dash address-history pagination uses a fixed page size; inconsistent totals, repeated transaction IDs or premature page termination produce an error instead of an apparently complete ledger. Explorer histories remain provider-reported data.

Core transaction input and output lists must be present. Amounts for the queried address must be exact nonnegative duffs; missing or malformed amounts stop the query before displaying or exporting a transfer direction. A valid zero remains zero. If an unrelated input or output has no known amount, the address flow can remain exact while the transaction fee is unavailable.

### Input and history integrity

Bitcoin addresses are decoded with Base58Check or the appropriate Bech32/Bech32m witness checksum, including network and program-length checks. Uniform uppercase Bech32 is accepted and normalized. EVM mixed-case addresses must pass EIP-55; lower/uppercase representations are accepted and canonicalized before batch deduplication. Account coin addresses must be in their coin's own form: a Solana base58 key of 32 bytes, an XRP classic address, a Stellar account ID (G…) or a Tron address with a valid checksum, NEAR takes the account's public key as wallets and the Deriver write it, `ed25519:` and base58 of 32 bytes, and reads the implicit account it names; 64 hex digits, the implicit account's own form, are refused as private material because they cannot be told from a private key, and named accounts such as name.near are not read. A Cosmos or Injective address is bech32 in either letter case, and Injective also takes the EVM address (0x…) of the same account. Hedera takes an account ID `0.0.N`, with or without its mainnet checksum (HIP-15, `0.0.N-abcde`; another network's checksum is refused), an EVM address, or a public key in DER, Ed25519 (`302a…`, bare or as the alias `0.0.302a…` the Deriver shows) or ECDSA (`302d…`, bare or as its alias); a raw key's 64 hex digits are refused as private material. TON takes a user-friendly address (`UQ…` or `EQ…`, in either base64 alphabet) or a raw one (`0:…`) of a mainnet basechain account and reads it as its non-bounceable form. Each coin's form is checked against a public value of the phrase (its first address or key, or for Hedera the account the network gave that key) and a refused variant at startup. An Ed25519 secret key in base58 (a Solana keypair, a NEAR secret key `ed25519:…`) or in hex (seed and public key, TON's 64-byte secret key), a private key in DER written in hex (Hedera's PKCS#8 and SEC1 keys) and a Stellar secret seed (S…) are refused as private material and erased, like a phrase or a WIF. The complete input batch is screened before any network request; detected private material is erased from both input fields.

Identity history reconciles transaction, transfer, document and contract counts with the Identity summary. Repeated record identities, overlapping pages, changed counts and premature end-of-history fail explicitly. Multiple transfer legs can share a transaction hash. The current Explorer response omits stable transfer row IDs, so indistinguishable legs across page boundaries are rejected as ambiguous rather than silently counted. Collection counts unavailable from both summary and pagination remain explicitly unverified; display limits remain visible.

Created-token supply is token metadata, not the Identity's owned balance or Platform credits. CSV/XLSX leave DASH amount columns empty for token-supply records and retain the token ID, atomic supply and decimals in metadata; JSON preserves the typed token fields.
